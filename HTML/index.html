A river inside a mountain

On the west coast of Palawan, a river runs through a limestone cave and comes out at the sea. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the New7Wonders of Nature. Scroll down or use the menu to explore the place.

About the place

The underground river is inside Puerto Princesa Subterranean River National Park, near Sabang. The St. Paul Mountain Range rises behind it, and the river flows into the West Philippine Sea.

The river is tidal, so the sea reaches far into the cave and the water rises and falls with it. The cave is a karst system, shaped over a very long time as rainwater slowly dissolved limestone. That is why it has so many columns, ridges and domes.

The park was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1999, and the river was named one of the New7Wonders of Nature in 2012.

Attractions

These are the main stops on the standard visit, in the order you reach them.

  1. Sabang Beach

    A white-sand beach facing the sea. The boat landing for the cave is here.

  2. The cave mouth

    A wide arch opens onto a lagoon. Daylight fades quickly once the boat goes under the rock.

  3. Stalactites and stalagmites

    Limestone shapes hang from the ceiling and rise from the walls. Guides name shapes such as the Holy Family.

  4. Italian's Chamber

    One of the biggest rooms on the tour, roughly 360 m long and 60 m high. Bats and swiftlets fill the dark above.

  5. The Cathedral

    Tall columns that look like a church interior. The boat turns here and heads back to the light.

Activities

Underground river tour

A paddle boat ride of about 45 minutes with an audio guide and a boatman who lights the formations.

Forest trail

Walk through lowland forest. You may see monitor lizards and long-tailed macaques. Do not feed them.

Beach time

Swim or relax at Sabang Beach before or after your tour.

Ask at Sabang or an official tour operator about other activities, since offers change.

Food and Culture

Food

Palawan is known for fresh seafood, such as grilled fish and kinilaw. Palawan cashew nuts are a popular gift to take home.

Culture

Palawan is home to Indigenous peoples, including the Tagbanua, Batak and Palaw'an. Be respectful, and ask before taking photos of people.

Travel Guide

Everything you need to plan a smooth trip.

Getting there

Take a van, bus or taxi from Puerto Princesa City to Sabang, about two hours by road. Many tours include the transfer.

Best time to go

The dry season, roughly February to May, has the calmest seas. Heavy rain or rough water can close the cave.

What to bring

Sunscreen, water, a hat, a light rain jacket, shoes with good grip and a camera.

Permit and booking

Entry needs a permit, and daily visitors are limited. Book ahead, and check the official rules and fees before you travel.

Tips: go in the morning, follow your boatman's instructions, do not touch the formations, and keep your mouth closed when you look up at the bats.

Accommodations

In Sabang

Simple beach resorts and guesthouses close to the cave. A good choice if you want an early tour.

In Puerto Princesa City

More hotels and restaurants to choose from. It is about two hours by road to Sabang.

Book early in the dry season, when more visitors come.

Events

Palawan holds local festivals during the year, such as the Baragatan Festival, which is usually in June in Puerto Princesa City.
